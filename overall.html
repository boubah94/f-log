🩺 What this means for your health

❤️ Heart — resting HR ~68, HRV ~46 ms

Resting heart rate averaged 67.8 this week (61–74 day to day). Normal is 60–100; trained athletes sit at 40–60. HRV averaged ~46 ms — a healthy range, and the number personal trainers actually watch: it rises when you're recovered and drops when you're run down.

So what: your heart is healthy-average, not yet athletic — and the lever that moves both numbers is an aerobic base. That's the missing Friday runs, not more lifting. Better sleep pushes HRV up within days.

👟 Movement — 13.5k weekday steps, 37.6 km this week

A 2022 Lancet Public Health meta-analysis (~47,000 adults) found mortality risk keeps dropping as daily steps rise, leveling off around 8–10k for under-60s. Your workdays clear that bar by half again — the Corning floor is quietly doing cardio's job.

So what: the weekday win is already banked. The hole is the off-day: Saturday was 1,864 steps. A ~6k floor on rest days keeps blood flow (and recovery) up without costing you the couch.

😴 Sleep — 5h30m weeknights is the red flag

Only 3 nights tracked, but both weeknights landed under 6 hours. The research here is blunt: one week of 5-hour nights cut testosterone 10–15% in young men (JAMA, 2011), and roughly 70% of daily growth hormone is released during deep sleep — which yours is actually good at (1h20m–1h47m when tracked).

So what: for a muscle-gain goal, sleep is a bigger lever than any program change. You're not failing at deep sleep — you're failing at time in bed. Thirty more minutes on worknights beats any supplement.

🫁 Recovery signals — all quiet, all normal

Respiratory rate ~15 breaths/min (normal 12–20), blood oxygen 94–97% on sampled days (normal 95–100), standing ~3.4 hrs/day, 32 flights of stairs this week. Nothing here needs fixing.

So what: these are your early-warning gauges. A respiratory rate or resting HR that jumps for 2–3 days straight usually means illness or overreaching before you feel it — worth knowing they're being watched.
