🩺 What this means for your health

❤️ Heart — resting HR ~66, HRV ~47 ms

Across all 7 days (Nov 9–15), resting heart rate ran 62–70 (avg ~66) and HRV 33.5–58.8 ms (avg ~47). Best day was Sunday Nov 15 — resting HR 62, HRV 58.8, after a full rest day. Worst was Tuesday Nov 10 — HRV 33.5, the day of the double session (legs at midnight, chest at noon). Both gauges sit in the healthy range and are flat vs the last two weeks (~65/~49, ~67/~47).

So what: seven days makes this a trend, and the trend is "steady, not improving" — the levers are the same as ever: an aerobic base (the missing runs) and sleep. The double-session dip is normal recovery, not a warning sign — it bounced back within a day.

👟 Movement — ~14.7k weekday steps, Saturday fixed, Sunday collapsed

Workdays ran 12,156 / 16,505 / 12,667 / 14,891 / 15,077 steps (avg ~14,659). Saturday Nov 14 hit 14,139 — the first Saturday over 4k in three weeks. Sunday Nov 15 managed 2,013 steps and 158 kcal — the week's only day under 4k, after a 9h39m sleep that ran to 2:12 PM. Total walking distance 27.6 mi over the week, all walking, no running. Research on ~47,000 adults (Lancet Public Health, 2022) puts the mortality-benefit plateau around 8–10k steps for under-60s — your workday floor cleared that bar every day.

So what: the Corning floor keeps quietly doing cardio's job, and Saturday is fixed — the collapse just moved to Sunday. A short walk on the rest day is still the cheapest win on this page.

😴 Sleep — 6h50m average, two short nights and a missing one

Five staged nights (mornings Nov 10, 11, 13, 14, 15) averaged 6h50m asleep with ~1h21m deep. Two nights fell under 6 hours (5h35m Nov 10, 4h25m Nov 14), and the Nov 12 morning has only a 2h55m unstaged block — that night is effectively missing from the data. The long nights are long (8h19m, 9h39m), so the average hides a swing from 4h25m to 9h39m. The JAMA (2011) finding stands: a week of 5-hour nights cuts testosterone 10–15% in young men, and most growth hormone arrives in deep sleep.

So what: the average is fine; the variance is the problem. Two short nights a week plus a missing one is what quietly caps recovery. One fixed lights-out time on worknights is worth more than any single lift this week.
